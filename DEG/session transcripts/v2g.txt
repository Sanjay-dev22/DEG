Real V2G pilots that have actually happened — not hypothetical

- China: 30 real pilot projects across 9 cities launched in 2025, targeting 5,000 V2G charging facilities by end of 2027.
- Netherlands: 500 bidirectional Renaults running in a car-sharing scheme in Utrecht — this one has actually graduated from pilot to a fully commercial service, the most mature real example found.
- USA: Maryland adopted the country's first full V2G interconnection rules (June 2025); Sunrun + Baltimore Gas & Electric then ran the first real residential V2G pilot using Ford F-150 Lightning trucks. Massachusetts is separately running a state-funded bidirectional-charging demo through Dec 2026.
- UK: Nissan completed a year-long real trial at the University of Nottingham and became the first automaker to get official grid-code certification (G99) for an AC-based V2G setup.

(Sources: IEA (https://www.iea.org/reports/vehicle-to-grid-technology), MassCEC (https://www.masscec.com/masscec-focus/clean-transportation/electric-vehicle-charging-infrastructure/vehicle-to-everything-demonstration), Nuvve 2026 Outlook (https://nuvve.com/2026-outlook-report/), IEEE YP blog (https://yp.ieee.org/blog/2025/12/24/v2g-in-2025-the-missing-piece-that-finally-connects-electromobility-and-smart-grids/))

Your Ather/Ola doubt — you're correct, and here's the important nuance

Confirmed directly: Ather Grid and Ola's charging network are both proprietary and closed — Ather uses its own connector design (though it has since opened the patent to other two-wheeler OEMs), and Ola's slot is incompatible even with Ather's own network. These are closed ecosystems; there's nothing public to study there.

But that's not the standard the rest of the industry (or India's public charging buildout) is actually converging on. The real open standard is called OCPP (Open Charge Point Protocol) — a vendor-neutral, publicly documented standard for how a charging station talks to its backend management software, regardless of who made the hardware. Directly relevant: "India is expanding its universal public charging network powered by OCPP-enabled chargers," in contrast to closed brand networks like Ather/Ola. This is what your team should actually study, not Ather or Ola's internals — those are dead ends for research purposes; OCPP is the real, open, documented thing.

The real ISO 15118-20 handshake, step by step

This is the actual conversation a car and a bidirectional charger have the moment you plug in:

1. SLAC — a low-level physical-layer handshake so the car and charger can even establish a digital link over the charging cable.
2. SDP (Service Discovery) — the car finds the charger's communication address.
3. TLS 1.3 security setup — mandatory for -20 (the older ISO 15118-2 only required TLS 1.2, and only for payment).
4. SupportedAppProtocolReq/Res — car and charger explicitly agree on which version of the standard they'll both speak.
5. SessionSetupReq — a formal session begins.
6. ... actual charging or discharging happens ...
7. SessionStopReq/Res — either side formally ends the session.

(Source: Trialog (https://www.trialog.com/en/iso-15118-20-not-only-a-further-step-towards-bidirectional-charging/), Typhoon HIL docs (https://www.typhoon-hil.com/documentation/typhoon-hil-software-manual/References/iso_15118_20_protocol.html))

(Source: https://github.com/SwitchEV/RISE-V2G, Eclipse RISE V2G (https://projects.eclipse.org/projects/iot.risev2g))

So, to directly answer "is my understanding off, or is the goal unclear"

Your understanding isn't off — V2G genuinely is both hardware and software, and it's fair to have been unsure which one this research track was even about. The honest scoping is: hardware (Bosch already has it) + car-to-charger protocol (ISO 15118-20, real, open, studyable via RISE-V2G) + charger-to-backend protocol (OCPP, real, open) + backend-to-market layer (OpenADR/Beckn/IES, still an open question for India specifically, which is what we've spent this whole thread researching). That's four distinct layers, not one confused thing — worth presenting to your team exactly that way.
